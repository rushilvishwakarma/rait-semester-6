CHAPTER 4
LEXICAL SEMANTICS


INTRODUCTION

Semantic Analysis is a subfield of Natural Language Processing (NLP) that focuses on understanding the meaning of natural language.

While understanding language appears effortless for humans, it is highly complex for machines due to ambiguity, subjectivity, and contextual dependence.

Semantic Analysis attempts to capture the meaning of text by considering:
- Context
- Logical structure of sentences
- Grammatical roles

Lexical Semantic Analysis focuses specifically on understanding the meanings of individual words in context.


LEXICAL SEMANTIC ANALYSIS

Lexical Semantic Analysis involves identifying the meaning that each word contributes to a text.

It typically corresponds to retrieving the dictionary meaning that a word is intended to carry in a given context.


WHAT DO WORDS MEAN?

Understanding individual word meaning depends on context.

Example:
- “Forward into the ocean.”
- “She was leaning forward.”

Although the word “forward” is the same, it carries different meanings depending on context.

Lexical semantics studies:
- Individual words in context
- How word meanings relate to one another
- Lexical relations between meanings


WHAT IS LEXICAL SEMANTICS?

Semantics:
- Study of linguistic meaning

Lexical Semantics:
- Study of word meaning
- Deals with a language’s lexicon (collection of words)
- Represents each word and its meaning
- Explains how word meanings are interrelated


BASIC PROCESS OF NATURAL LANGUAGE UNDERSTANDING (NLU)

Natural Language Understanding involves multiple stages:
- Lexical analysis
- Syntactic analysis
- Semantic analysis
- Discourse and pragmatic interpretation


MEANING IN LANGUAGE

Meaning in language is traditionally studied at three levels:
- Meaning of discourse or text
- Meaning of individual sentences or utterances
- Meaning of individual words

Lexical semantics focuses on the word level, which supports higher-level sentence and discourse interpretation.


WORD MEANING

In earlier models:
- Verbs provided predicate-argument structures
- Nouns were treated as constants

However, word meaning involves more complexity, motivating lexical semantic analysis.


PRELIMINARIES

What is a word?
- Types and tokens
- Stems and roots
- Inflected forms

Lexeme:
- An entry in the lexicon
- Consists of a form and a single meaning representation

Lexicon:
- A collection of lexemes

A lexeme includes:
- Orthographic form
- Phonological form
- Meaning representation (sense)

Example dictionary entries:
- red (noun): the color of blood or a ruby
- blood (noun): the red liquid circulating in animals


RELATIONS AMONG LEXEMES AND THEIR SENSES

Major lexical relations include:
- Homonymy
- Synonymy
- Polysemy
- Metonymy
- Hyponymy / Hypernymy
- Meronymy
- Antonymy


HOMONYMY

Homonymy occurs when lexemes share the same form but have unrelated meanings.

They may share:
- Pronunciation
- Spelling
- Or both

Example:
- bat (wooden stick)
- bat (flying mammal)


SYNONYMY

Synonymy refers to different words expressing related meanings.

Examples:
- cat, feline, Siamese cat

True synonymy is rare:
- Words often differ in usage
- Carry different implications
- Appear in different contexts


POLYSEMY

Polysemy occurs when a single word has multiple related meanings.

Example:
- bank (financial institution)
- bank (building)
- bank (place where money is stored)

Example:
- “That dog has floppy ears.”
- “He has a good ear for jokes.”

Polysemy differs from homonymy because meanings are related.


METONYMY

Metonymy uses one aspect of an entity to refer to the whole.

Example:
- “The White House released new figures today.”

This implies the people within the White House, not the building itself.


HYPONYMY AND HYPERNYMY

ISA relationship between concepts.

Examples:
- hyponym(robin, bird)
- hyponym(bird, animal)
- hyponym(emus, bird)

Definitions:
- A is a hypernym of B if B is a type of A
- A is a hyponym of B if A is a type of B


BASIC-LEVEL CATEGORIES

Folk biological categorization:

- Unique beginner: plant, animal
- Life form: tree, bush, flower
- Generic (basic) level: pine, oak, maple
- Specific level: white pine, ponderosa pine
- Varietal level: western ponderosa pine

The basic level:
- Corresponds roughly to genus
- Is the most cognitively salient
- Aligns best with scientific taxonomy


MERONYMY

Meronymy represents part–whole relationships.

Examples:
- part-of(beak, bird)
- part-of(bark, tree)

Meronymy is conceptually transitive but not always lexically acceptable.

Example:
- The knob is part of the door.
- The door is part of the house.
- ? The knob is part of the house.


ANTONYMY

Antonymy represents lexical opposites.

Examples:
- antonym(large, small)
- antonym(big, small)
- antonym(big, little)

However, not all size-related pairs are antonyms (large ≠ little).


THESAURI AND LEXICAL RELATIONS

- Polysemy: same word, different senses
- Synonymy: different words, related senses

Thesauri:
- Group related words
- Define relationships similar to lexical relations
- Support semantic organization of vocabulary


ONTOLOGY

Ontology (general definition):
- A branch of metaphysics concerned with the nature of being

In computational linguistics:
- A structured organization of concepts
- Defines what exists in a domain
- Does not necessarily specify all relations

Related terms:
- Taxonomy
- Dictionary
- Category structure

Ontologies often function like advanced thesauri.


LEXICAL RESOURCES

Common lexical resources include:
- Word lists
- Online dictionaries
- Corpora

The most ambitious lexical resource is WordNet.


WORDNET

WordNet is:
- A lexical database of English
- Organized around synsets (sets of synonyms)

A synset represents a single concept and includes:
- Synonyms
- A definition (gloss)
- Usage examples

WordNet contains separate databases for:
- Nouns
- Verbs
- Adjectives
- Adverbs

Example:
Verb sense of “table” meaning “to defer”:
{postpone, hold over, table, shelve, set back, defer, remit, put off}

For WordNet, the meaning of a word sense is defined by its synset.


WORDNET STRUCTURE AND RELATIONS

WordNet encodes lexical relations such as:
- Synonymy
- Hypernymy
- Hyponymy
- Meronymy
- Antonymy

These relations organize concepts into semantic networks.


WORDNET USAGE

WordNet is publicly available online and widely used for:
- Semantic analysis
- Word sense disambiguation
- Lexical research
- NLP applications
