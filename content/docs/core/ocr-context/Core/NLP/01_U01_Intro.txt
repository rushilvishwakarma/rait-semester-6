INTRODUCTION TO NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING


Natural Language

Refers to the language spoken by people,
e.g. English, Hindi, Marathi, Spanish, French, etc.,
as opposed to artificial programming languages, like C, C++, Java, etc.


Natural Language Processing

Applications that deal with these natural languages in a way or another.


INTRODUCTION TO NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

The process of computer analysis of input provided in a human language (natural language), and conversion of this input into a useful form of representation.

The field of NLP is primarily concerned with getting computers to perform useful and interesting tasks with human languages.

The field of NLP is secondarily concerned with helping us come to a better understanding of human language.


DEFINITION NLP

The sub-domain of artificial intelligence concerned with the task of developing programs possessing some capability of ‘understanding’ a natural language in order to achieve some specific goal.

A transformation from one representation (the input text) to another (internal representation).

Understanding.


FORMS OF NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

The inputoutput of an NLP system can be
- Written text
- Speech

During classes, our focus will be mainly on written text (not speech).

To process written text, we need
- Lexical, syntactic, semantic knowledge about the language
- Discourse information, real world knowledge

To process spoken language, we need everything required to process written text, plus the challenges of speech recognition and speech synthesis.


WHY NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Huge amounts of data.
Internet = at least 20 billion pages.
Applications for processing large amounts of texts require NLP expertise.

The applications in our day-to-day life are the obvious motivation.


COMPONENTS OF NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Natural Language Understanding
Mapping the given input in the natural language into a useful representation.
Different levels of analysis required
- Morphological analysis
- Syntactic analysis
- Semantic analysis
- Discourse analysis

Natural Language Generation
Producing output in the natural language from some internal representation.
Different levels of synthesis required
- Deep planning (what to say)
- Syntactic generation


GOAL OF NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Engineering Goal
Design, implement, and test systems that process natural languages (e.g., English, Hindi) for practical applications.

STAGES OF NLP
STAGES OF NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING


STAGES  LEVELS OF NLP

Morphological Analysis
Individual words are analyzed into their components.

Syntactic Analysis
Linear sequences of words are transformed into structures that show how the words relate to each other.

Semantic Analysis
A transformation is made from the input text to an internal representation that reflects the meaning.

Discourse Analysis
Resolving references between sentences.

Pragmatic Analysis
To reinterpret what was said to what was actually meant.


STEPS OF NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Morphology
Concerns the way words are built up from smaller meaning-bearing units.
Example truth + ful + ness

Syntax
Concerns how words are put together to form correct sentences and what structural role each word has.
Example the dog ate my homework

Semantics
Concerns what words mean and how these meanings combine in sentences.
Example plant (industrial plant  living organism)


STEPS OF NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING (CONTINUED)

Pragmatics
Concerns how sentences are used in different situations and how it affects interpretation.

Discourse
Concerns how preceding sentences affect interpretation of the next sentence.

MORPHOLOGY

A morpheme is the smallest unit of language that has a distinctive meaning.
This includes words, prefixes, suffixes, etc.

Morphology is the study of the structure and meaning of morphemes.
Rules and syntaxes govern how morphemes combine.

LECTURE NO 3
CHALLENGES OF NLP


WHY NL UNDERSTANDING IS HARD

Natural language is rich and ambiguous.
Issues include
- Representing meaning
- Mapping structures to meanings
- Lexical ambiguity
- Syntactic ambiguity
- Pronoun interpretation
- Contextual influence
- Same meaning expressed differently
- Unclear interaction among components


AMBIGUITY

Ambiguity is the ability to be understood in more than one way.

Types of Ambiguity
- Lexical
- Syntactic
- Semantic
- Pragmatic


LEXICAL AMBIGUITY

Example
silver as noun, adjective, verb
She won two silver medals.
She made a silver speech.
Her worries had silvered her hair.


SYNTACTIC AMBIGUITY

Sentence parsed in different ways.
Example
The chicken is ready to eat.


SEMANTIC AMBIGUITY

Example
The car hit the pole while it was moving.

Possible interpretations
- Car was moving
- Pole was moving


PHONETIC AMBIGUITY

[ralt] = write, right, rite
