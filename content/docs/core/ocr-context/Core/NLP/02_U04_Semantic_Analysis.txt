LECTURE 22
SEMANTIC ATTACHMENTS FOR FRAGMENTS OF ENGLISH


SEMANTIC ATTACHMENTS

Semantic attachments specify how to compute the meaning representation of a construction from the meanings of its constituent parts.

They define the meaning representation assigned to grammatical constructions.


ATTACHMENTS FOR SMALL ENGLISH FRAGMENTS

- Sentence
- Noun Phrase (NP)
- Verb Phrase (VP)
- Prepositional Phrase (PP)


ATTACHMENTS FOR ENGLISH SENTENCES

Example sentences:
- Flight 487 serves lunch.
- Serve lunch.
- Does Flight 207 serve lunch?
- Which flights serve lunch?

All meaning representations contain propositions related to serving lunch on flights.


DECLARATIVE SENTENCES

Operators applied to First Order Predicate Calculus (FOPC):

- DCL: Declarative
- IMP: Imperative
- YNQ: Yes–No Question
- WHQ: WH Question

Example:
Flight 487 serves lunch.

Grammar:
S → NP VP  
Semantic Attachment:
DCL(VP.sem(NP.sem))

Interpretation:
A factual statement added to the knowledge base.


GRAMMAR RULES WITH SEMANTIC ATTACHMENTS

S → NP VP  
{DCL(VP.sem(NP.sem))}

S → VP  
{IMP(VP.sem(DummyYou))}

S → Aux NP VP  
{YNQ(VP.sem(NP.sem))}

S → WhWord NP VP  
{WHQ(NP.sem.var, VP.sem(NP.sem))}

Nom → Noun Nom  
{λx Nom.sem(x) ∧ NN(Noun.sem)}

PP → P NP  
{P.sem(NP.sem)}

P → on  
{λyλx On(x,y)}

Det → a  
{∃}

Nom → N  
{λx Isa(x, N.sem)}


IMPERATIVE SENTENCES

Imperative sentences:
- Begin with a verb phrase
- Do not have an explicit subject

Example:
Serve lunch.

Semantic representation:
IMP(∃e Serving(e) ∧ Server(e, DummyYou) ∧ Served(e, Lunch))

Imperatives are treated as speech acts requesting action.


YES–NO QUESTIONS

Example:
Does Flight 207 serve lunch?

Grammar:
S → Aux NP VP

Semantic representation:
YNQ(∃e Serving(e) ∧ Server(e, Flt207) ∧ Served(e, Lunch))


WH-QUESTIONS

Example:
Which flights serve lunch?

Grammar:
S → WhWord NP VP

Semantic representation:
WHQ(x, ∃e Serving(e) ∧ Server(e, x) ∧ Served(e, Lunch) ∧ Isa(x, Flight))


NOUN PHRASES: COMPOUND NOMINALS

Examples:
- Flight schedule
- Summer flight schedule

Semantic representation:
λx Isa(x, Schedule) ∧ NN(x, Flight)
λx Isa(x, Schedule) ∧ NN(x, Flight) ∧ NN(x, Summer)


GENITIVE NOUN PHRASES

Examples:
- Atlanta’s airport
- Maharani’s menu

Semantic representation:
∃x Isa(x, Airport) ∧ GN(x, Atlanta)


ADJECTIVE PHRASES

Examples:
- cheap restaurant
- this restaurant is cheap

Simple intersective semantics often fails:
- small elephant
- former friend
- fake gun

Correct approach:
Represent adjectival modification using a general modification relation.

Nominal → Adj Nominal  
{λx Nom.sem(x) ∧ AM(x, Adj.sem)}

Example:
λx Isa(x, Restaurant) ∧ AM(x, Cheap)


INFINITIVE VERB PHRASES

Example:
I told Harry to go to Maharani.

Representation involves two events:
- Telling event
- Going event

Harry participates in both events.

Key issues:
- Event structure
- Shared arguments across events


PREPOSITIONAL PHRASES (PP)

Functions of PPs:
1. Assert binary relations
2. Signal arguments of verbs

PP roles:
- NP modifiers
- VP modifiers
- VP arguments


PP AS NOMINAL MODIFIERS

Example:
A restaurant on Pearl

Semantic representation:
∃x Isa(x, Restaurant) ∧ On(x, Pearl)


PP AS VP MODIFIERS

Example:
ate dinner in a hurry

Semantic representation:
∃e Eating(e) ∧ Eater(e, y) ∧ Eaten(e, Dinner) ∧ In(e, ∃h Hurry(h))


LECTURE 23
RELATIONS AMONG LEXEMES AND THEIR SENSES


THREE PERSPECTIVES ON MEANING

Lexical Semantics:
- Meaning of individual words

Formal (Compositional) Semantics:
- Meaning of sentences

Discourse / Pragmatics:
- Meaning in context and interaction


PRELIMINARIES

Lexeme:
- A pairing of a form with a single meaning

Lexicon:
- Collection of lexemes

Lemma:
- Canonical uninflected form
- Part-of-speech specific

Lemmatization:
- Mapping word forms to lemmas


SEMANTIC RELATIONSHIPS

- Homonymy
- Polysemy
- Synonymy
- Antonymy
- Hypernymy
- Hyponymy
- Meronymy
- Holonymy


HOMONYMY

Lexemes with:
- Same form
- Unrelated meanings

Examples:
- bank (river) vs bank (financial)
- bat (animal) vs bat (sports equipment)

Causes issues in:
- Text-to-speech
- Information retrieval
- Machine translation
- Speech recognition


POLYSEMY

Single lexeme with multiple related meanings.

Examples:
- bank (building, institution, service)
- food bank

Polysemy:
- Very common
- More frequent in verbs
- Often hard to distinguish from homonymy


SYNONYMY

Words with similar meanings in some contexts.

Examples:
- couch / sofa
- big / large
- car / automobile

Perfect synonymy is rare due to:
- Register
- Politeness
- Usage constraints


ANTONYMY

Opposite senses along a dimension.

Examples:
- hot / cold
- long / short
- up / down

Types:
- Binary opposites
- Gradable opposites
- Reversives (rise/fall)


HYPONYMY AND HYPERNYMY

Hyponym:
- More specific sense

Hypernym:
- More general sense

Examples:
- car → vehicle
- mango → fruit
- dog → animal

Hyponymy properties:
- Entailment
- Transitivity


MERONYMY AND HOLONYMY

Part–whole relations.

Examples:
- wheel → car
- nose → face


LECTURE 24
WORDNET


WORDNET OVERVIEW

WordNet is:
- A hierarchical lexical database
- An online thesaurus with dictionary features

Lexical categories:
- Nouns
- Verbs
- Adjectives
- Adverbs


WORDNET ACCESS

Available online at:
http://wordnetweb.princeton.edu/perl/webwn


WORDNET ENTRY FORMAT

Example: bass (noun)

Senses include:
- Musical range
- Singer
- Musical instrument
- Fish

Also includes adjective senses.


WORDNET NOUN RELATIONS

- Hypernym
- Hyponym
- Meronym
- Holonym
- Antonym
- Instance relations


WORDNET VERB RELATIONS

- Hypernym
- Troponym
- Entailment
- Antonym


WORDNET HIERARCHIES

Word senses organized in inheritance chains.

Example:
bass (singer) → musician → performer → person → entity

bass (instrument) → instrument → artifact → physical object → entity


SENSES AND SYNSETS

A WordNet sense is represented by a synset:
- Set of near-synonyms sharing a meaning

Example:
chump, fool, gull, patsy, sucker

All share the same gloss:
“A person who is gullible and easy to take advantage of”
