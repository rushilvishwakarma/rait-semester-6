## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: WHAT IS DOE?

Design of Experiments (DoE) is a technique for exploring, estimating, and confirming relationships between input variables (factors) and output performance (response).

- EXPLORE: Understanding the data structure from the process.  
- ESTIMATE: Determining the specific effects of process variables.  
- CONFIRM: Verifying predicted results through statistical validation.

## THE ENEMY OF EFFICIENCY: OVAT

The One-Variable-At-a-Time Approach  

The traditional approach involves varying one variable while keeping others fixed, then moving to the next.  

This method depends on guesswork, luck, and intuition.

### KEY FLAWS

- Resource Heavy: Requires large resources to obtain limited information.  
- False Optima: Often misses the true optimal condition.  
- No Interaction Data: Cannot detect how factors influence each other.

## THE CRITICAL FLAW: INTERACTIONS

When the effect of Factor A depends on Factor B.  

An interaction exists when the effect of one factor (e.g., Pressure) on the response depends on the level of another factor (e.g., Temperature).

- The Consequence: OVAT assumes independence. In complex systems, this leads to failure.

**[Image Description]**:  
- No interaction: parallel lines (Temp1, Temp2 vs Pressure)  
- Interaction: crossing lines showing dependency

## THREE PILLARS OF EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

### 1. Randomization

- Definition: Running trials in a random order rather than a set sequence.  
- Statistical Purpose: To distribute the effect of extraneous, uncontrolled factors (noise) evenly. Ensures observations are independently distributed random variables.

---

### 2. Replication

- Definition: Repetition of the entire experiment on different units.  
- Statistical Purpose: To estimate experimental error (pure error) and determine if differences are statistically significant.

---

### 3. Blocking

- Definition: A design technique to group homogeneous experimental units.  
- Statistical Purpose: To eliminate the effect of nuisance factors (e.g., batches, shifts) from experimental error.  
- Result: Increases precision of comparisons.

## MEASUREMENT SYSTEMS

The prerequisite to valid statistics.  

Before DoE, we must validate the measurement system.

- Repeatability: Same person, same part  
- Reproducibility: Different people, same part

## ANALYSIS OF VARIANCE (ANOVA)

Partitioning the Variation.

\[
SS(\text{Total}) = SS(\text{Factors}) + SS(\text{Error})
\]

- Total variation in dataset  
- Signal: variation due to factors  
- Noise: variation due to randomness  

**F-Ratio = Signal / Noise**  
Large F → significant factor

## VALIDATING ASSUMPTIONS

### Normal Probability Plot

DoE assumes residuals are normally distributed.

- Success: points follow straight line  
- Failure: S-shape or outliers

## SCREENING EFFECTS

### Half-Normal Plot

- Uses absolute effects

## HYPOTHESIS TESTING

Using t-test for coefficients:

\[
t_0 = \frac{\text{Coefficient}}{\text{Standard Error}}
\]

Reject H₀ if:
\[
|t_0| > t(\text{critical})
\]

- High t → significant variable  
- Low t → noise
