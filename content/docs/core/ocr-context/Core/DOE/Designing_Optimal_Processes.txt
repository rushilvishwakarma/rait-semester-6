# Industrial Experimentation: From Guesswork to Science

## Traditional vs DOE
- Traditional: trial-and-error, one variable at a time  
- DOE: structured, simultaneous experimentation  

**[Image Description]**: Comparison of two approaches—trial-and-error vs structured experimental design

## Manufacturing Process as a System

- Inputs:
  - **Controllable**: Speed, Pressure, Temperature  
  - **Uncontrollable**: Humidity, Noise, Ambient Temp  
- Output: Yield, Strength, Finish  

**[Image Description]**: System box with inputs (left) → process → outputs (right)

**Key Insight**: Identify “vital few” from “trivial many”

---

## OVAT (One Variable At a Time) – Problem

- Changes one factor while holding others constant  
- Misses interaction effects  
- Leads to **local optimum**, not global  

**[Image Description]**: Contour plot showing path stuck at local optimum while true optimum lies elsewhere

---

## DOE (Simultaneous Approach)

**Definition**: Change multiple factors together to study effect on response  

### Objectives
- Exploration → understand behavior  
- Estimation → quantify effects  
- Confirmation → validate results  

**[Image Description]**: Flow showing exploration → estimation → confirmation

---

## Interaction Effects

### Scenario A: No Interaction
- Lines parallel → effect independent  

### Scenario B: Strong Interaction
- Lines cross → effect depends on other factor  

**[Image Description]**: Two graphs—parallel lines vs crossing lines (interaction present)

**Insight**: Real systems often behave like Scenario B

---

## Business Value of DOE

- Increase yield & stability  
- Reduce time to market  
- Lower cost (scrap, rework)  
- Improve process understanding  

---

## Experiment Basics

### Factors
- Input variables (e.g., temperature)

### Levels
- Settings (low/high)

### Response
- Output (e.g., strength)

### Design Matrix
- Table of trials (combinations of factors)

---

## Principle 1: Randomization

- Random order of experiments  
- Avoid bias from time-based noise  

**[Image Description]**: Ordered vs randomized runs showing noise averaging

---

## Principle 2: Replication vs Repetition

- **Replication**: full rerun → captures real variation  
- **Repetition**: repeated measurement → captures measurement error  

**[Image Description]**:  
- Replication: setup → run → measure (repeat entire process)  
- Repetition: one run → measure multiple times  

**Key Insight**: Replication proves effect is real

---

## Principle 3: Blocking

- Group similar conditions (e.g., Batch A, Batch B)  
- Removes nuisance variation from error  

**[Image Description]**: Two blocks with averages compared; batch variation removed

---

## Measurement System Capability

- Must be reliable before experimentation  

### Gage R&R
- Repeatability (equipment)  
- Reproducibility (operator)  

### P/T Ratio
- < 10% → good  
- > 30% → unacceptable  

**[Image Description]**: Targets showing precise vs accurate measurement

---

## Selecting Quality Characteristics

### Prefer Continuous Data
- More information  
- Smaller sample size  

### Avoid Attribute Data
- Binary (pass/fail)  
- Less informative  

**Rule**: Convert attribute → measurable if possible

---

## Design Resolution

### Full Factorial
- High cost, full detail  

### Fractional Factorial
- Lower cost, some confounding  

| Resolution | Meaning |
|-----------|--------|
| III | Main effects confounded |
| IV | Main clear, interactions confounded |
| V | Main + interactions clear |

---

## Skills Required

- Engineering (process knowledge)  
- Statistical (design, analysis)  
- Planning (resources, timeline)  
- Teamwork  

**Key Insight**: 80% planning, 20% analysis

---

## Summary: Process Optimization

1. Hypothesis  
2. Experiment (randomized, replicated, blocked)  
3. Analyze interactions and effects
