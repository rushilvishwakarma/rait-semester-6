# Distance and Similarity Measures

Distance measures quantify dissimilarity; similarity measures quantify closeness.

## 1) Euclidean Distance (L2)

<img src="/images/docs/BDA/IA2/topic-4/diagram-01-euclidean-general.png" alt="Euclidean geometry overview" style={{maxWidth: '360px', margin: '0 auto', display: 'block'}} />

For points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$:

$$
d_E = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}
$$

For $n$ dimensions:

$$
d_E(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-y_i)^2}
$$

Example for points $(1,2)$ and $(5,6)$:

<img src="/images/docs/BDA/IA2/topic-4/diagram-02-euclidean-worked-example.png" alt="Euclidean worked example" style={{maxWidth: '360px', margin: '0 auto', display: 'block'}} />

$$
d_E = \sqrt{(5-1)^2 + (6-2)^2} = \sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}
$$

## 2) Manhattan Distance (L1)

<img src="/images/docs/BDA/IA2/topic-4/diagram-04-manhattan-path.png" alt="Manhattan path interpretation" style={{maxWidth: '360px', margin: '0 auto', display: 'block'}} />

For points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$:

$$
d_M = |x_2-x_1| + |y_2-y_1|
$$

Example from $(1,2)$ to $(3,5)$:

$$
d_M = |3-1| + |5-2| = 2 + 3 = 5
$$

## 4) Cosine Similarity and Cosine Distance

<img src="/images/docs/BDA/IA2/topic-4/diagram-10-cosine-vector-angle.png" alt="Cosine vector angle" style={{maxWidth: '340px', margin: '0 auto', display: 'block'}} />

Cosine similarity compares direction, not magnitude:

$$
\cos(\theta)=\frac{\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B}}{\|\mathbf{A}\|\|\mathbf{B}\|}
$$

Cosine distance:

$$
d_{\cos}=1-\cos(\theta)
$$

Special cases:

- $\cos(0^\circ)=1$ means maximum similarity.
- $\cos(90^\circ)=0$ means orthogonal (no directional similarity).

Example with $\mathbf{A}=[1,2,0]$ and $\mathbf{B}=[2,1,1]$:

<img src="/images/docs/BDA/IA2/topic-4/diagram-11-genre-axis-example.png" alt="Genre axis example for cosine similarity" style={{maxWidth: '340px', margin: '0 auto', display: 'block'}} />

$$
\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B}=4,
\quad \|\mathbf{A}\|=\sqrt{5},
\quad \|\mathbf{B}\|=\sqrt{6}
$$

$$
\cos(\theta)=\frac{4}{\sqrt{30}}\approx 0.73,
\quad d_{\cos}\approx 0.27
$$

## 5) Jaccard Similarity and Distance

For sets $A$ and $B$:

$$
J(A,B)=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|}
$$

$$
d_J(A,B)=1-J(A,B)
$$

Given:

$$
A=\{1,2,4,5\},\quad B=\{2,3,5,7\}
$$

$$
A\cap B=\{2,5\},\quad A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4,5,7\}
$$

$$
J(A,B)=\frac{2}{6}=0.333\ldots,
\quad d_J(A,B)=0.666\ldots
$$

## 6) Which Measure to Use

- Euclidean: dense numeric features where straight-line geometry is meaningful.
- Manhattan: grid-like movement or absolute coordinate differences.
- Cosine: text vectors and high-dimensional sparse vectors.
- Jaccard: set or binary-presence features.

These metrics are commonly used in KNN, clustering, recommendation, and retrieval.
