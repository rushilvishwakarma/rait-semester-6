### Q1. What is Big Data? Discuss different sources that generate big data. / What is Big data? Explain types of Big data. <QuestionDot color="cyan" text="Also in MSE-QB" />

<Highlight color="yellow">**Big Data**</Highlight> refers to extremely large and complex datasets that are generated at a very high speed and <Highlight color="red">cannot be efficiently processed using traditional data processing systems</Highlight> such as <DatabaseBadge type="rdbms" text="relational databases" />. As digital technologies have advanced, data is being produced continuously from multiple sources, making it <Highlight color="red">difficult for conventional systems to store, manage, and analyze</Highlight> it effectively.

The data becomes "Big Data" when its <Highlight color="blue">**size**, **speed of generation**, or **complexity**</Highlight> exceeds the capacity of traditional systems. Earlier systems were designed to handle structured data in limited volumes, whereas Big Data includes <Highlight color="blue">structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data</Highlight> such as text, images, videos, and sensor data.

Big Data plays a crucial role in modern organizations because it enables <Highlight color="green">advanced analytics and data-driven decision-making</Highlight>. By analyzing Big Data, organizations can <Highlight color="green">identify hidden patterns, predict trends, and gain deeper insights</Highlight> into customer behavior and business operations.

**Sources of Big Data:**

Big Data is generated from a wide range of sources. One major source is <Highlight color="yellow">**banking and credit card transactions**</Highlight>, where millions of financial records are generated every second. Another significant source is <Highlight color="yellow">**social media platforms**</Highlight> such as Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram — generating enormous amounts of unstructured data. <Highlight color="yellow">**Web and e-commerce systems**</Highlight> also contribute heavily by recording user clicks, searches, and purchase histories.

Additionally, <Highlight color="yellow">**mobile devices**</Highlight>, <Highlight color="yellow">**sensor technologies**</Highlight>, and <Highlight color="blue">**Internet of Things (IoT) networks**</Highlight> generate continuous streams of real-time data. <Highlight color="yellow">**Scientific instruments**</Highlight> and research experiments also produce large datasets.

### Q2. How much data does it take to be called Big Data? With example explain various data measures.

In Big Data analytics, data is measured using hierarchical storage units. The smallest unit is a <Highlight color="yellow">**bit**</Highlight>, and eight bits form one <Highlight color="yellow">**byte**</Highlight>.

| Unit | Equivalence | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Kilobyte (KB)** | 1024 Bytes | A short text file |
| **Megabyte (MB)** | 1024 KB | An MP3 song |
| **Gigabyte (GB)** | 1024 MB | A movie file |
| **Terabyte (TB)** | 1024 GB | Large corporate database |
| **Petabyte (PB)** | 1024 TB | Facebook daily data |
| **Exabyte (EB)** | 1024 PB | Global internet traffic per month |
| **Zettabyte (ZB)** | 1024 EB | All data created in a year globally |
| **Yottabyte (YB)** | 1024 ZB | Theoretical large-scale data |

In Big Data environments, data is commonly measured in <Highlight color="blue">**petabytes (PB)**, **exabytes (EB)**, **zettabytes (ZB)**, and **yottabytes (YB)**</Highlight>. Traditional data processing systems <Highlight color="red">struggle to handle such massive volumes</Highlight>, which is why Big Data technologies are required.

### Q3. With example explain types of Big Data. / What is Big data? Explain types of Big data. <QuestionDot color="cyan" text="Also in MSE-QB" />

Big Data is classified into three types based on its format and organization:

#### **1. <Highlight color="yellow">Structured Data</Highlight>**

- **Definition:** Data that has a <Highlight color="green">fixed schema</Highlight> and is <Highlight color="green">organized</Highlight> into a <Highlight color="green">tabular format</Highlight> (rows and columns).
- **Key Point:** It is <Highlight color="blue">easily searchable</Highlight> and <Highlight color="blue">managed using <DatabaseBadge type="rdbms" /></Highlight> (Relational Database Management Systems).
- **Example:** SQL databases, Excel sheets, and Employee Payroll records.

#### **2. <Highlight color="yellow">Semi-Structured Data</Highlight>**

- **Definition:** Data that does <Highlight color="red">not have a formal structure</Highlight> like a table but contains <Highlight color="green">tags or markers</Highlight> to separate data elements.
- **Key Point:** It is <Highlight color="blue">"self-describing"</Highlight> and sits between structured and unstructured data.
- **Example:** **XML** files, **JSON** files (used in web APIs), and CSV files.

#### **3. <Highlight color="yellow">Unstructured Data</Highlight>**

- **Definition:** Data that has <Highlight color="red">no predefined data model</Highlight> or <Highlight color="red">organizational structure</Highlight>.
- **Key Point:** This is the <Highlight color="blue">most common form of Big Data</Highlight> (approx. 80%) and is the <Highlight color="red">most difficult to analyze</Highlight>.
- **Example:** Social media posts, YouTube videos, Satellite images, and PDF documents.

### Q4. What are the 5Vs of big data? Explain. / Explain Big data characteristics. / What are 5vs in Big Data / Explain Big Data characteristics and its types. Discuss about its challenges. <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" /> <QuestionDot color="cyan" text="Also in MSE-QB" />

<Callout title="Very Important"></Callout>

Big Data is defined by five key characteristics commonly known as the <Highlight>**5 V's of Big Data**</Highlight>. These characteristics distinguish Big Data from traditional data systems.

The first characteristic is <Highlight color="yellow">**Volume**</Highlight>, which refers to the massive amount of data generated from various sources such as social media, sensors, and online transactions. Data today is generated in terabytes and petabytes, <Highlight color="blue">far exceeding the capacity of traditional systems</Highlight>.

The second characteristic is <Highlight color="yellow">**Velocity**</Highlight>, which indicates the <Highlight color="blue">speed at which data is generated, transmitted, and processed</Highlight>. Examples include real-time data from stock markets, social media feeds, and IoT devices.

The third characteristic is <Highlight color="yellow">**Variety**</Highlight>, which represents the <Highlight color="blue">different forms of data</Highlight> such as structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data. Handling such diverse data types is a major challenge in Big Data analytics.

The fourth characteristic is <Highlight color="yellow">**Veracity**</Highlight>, which <Highlight color="blue">deals with the quality, accuracy, and reliability of data</Highlight>. Big Data often contains <Highlight color="red">noise, inconsistencies, and uncertainty</Highlight>.

The fifth characteristic is <Highlight color="yellow">**Value**</Highlight>, which refers to the <Highlight color="blue">ability to extract meaningful and useful insights</Highlight> from raw data. Without value, large volumes of data have no practical significance.

| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

### Q9. What is Hadoop? Explain Hadoop architecture with features and core components.

<Highlight color="yellow">**Hadoop**</Highlight> is an <Highlight color="blue">open-source framework</Highlight> used for the <Highlight color="blue">distributed storage and processing of very large datasets</Highlight> using clusters of commodity computers. It is widely used in big data applications because it can <Highlight color="green">manage huge volumes of structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data</Highlight> in a reliable and scalable way.

Hadoop follows a <Highlight color="yellow">**master-slave architecture**</Highlight>. The <Highlight color="green">master node manages metadata and system resources</Highlight>, while the <Highlight color="blue">slave nodes store the actual data and perform the processing tasks</Highlight>. The architecture is mainly built around three working layers — storage, resource management, and processing — implemented through HDFS, YARN, and MapReduce.

**Core components of Hadoop**

- <Highlight color="yellow">**Hadoop Common**</Highlight> provides the <Highlight color="blue">basic libraries and utilities</Highlight> required by all other Hadoop modules.
- <Highlight color="yellow">**HDFS (Hadoop Distributed File System)**</Highlight> is the <Highlight color="green">storage layer</Highlight>. It divides large files into blocks and stores them across multiple DataNodes, making storage <Highlight color="green">distributed and fault tolerant</Highlight>.
- <Highlight color="yellow">**YARN (Yet Another Resource Negotiator)**</Highlight> is the <Highlight color="green">resource management layer</Highlight>. It allocates CPU and memory resources and schedules jobs efficiently.
- <Highlight color="yellow">**MapReduce**</Highlight> is the <Highlight color="green">processing layer</Highlight>. It processes large datasets in parallel by dividing the work into Map and Reduce tasks.

**Features of Hadoop**

- Hadoop is highly <Highlight color="green">**scalable**</Highlight> because more nodes can be added easily when data grows.
- It is <Highlight color="green">**fault tolerant**</Highlight> because data blocks are replicated on multiple machines.
- It supports <Highlight color="green">**distributed storage**</Highlight> and <Highlight color="green">**parallel processing**</Highlight>.
- It offers <Highlight color="blue">**high throughput**</Highlight> for batch jobs and is <Highlight color="green">cost-effective</Highlight> because it works on commodity hardware.

**Conclusion**

Thus, Hadoop architecture combines <Highlight color="blue">**HDFS for storage, YARN for resource management, and MapReduce for distributed processing**</Highlight>.

### Q11. What is HDFS? Explain HDFS architecture.

<Highlight color="yellow">**HDFS (Hadoop Distributed File System)**</Highlight> is the <Highlight color="blue">storage component of Hadoop</Highlight>. It is designed to store very large files across many machines in a <Highlight color="green">distributed and fault-tolerant</Highlight> manner. HDFS is optimized for <Highlight color="green">high throughput</Highlight> and is well suited for big data applications.

HDFS follows a <Highlight color="yellow">**master-slave architecture**</Highlight>. Files are split into large blocks, and these blocks are stored across multiple DataNodes. To ensure fault tolerance, each block is <Highlight color="green">replicated on more than one machine</Highlight>. This means that even if one node fails, the data can still be recovered from another node.

**Components of HDFS**

- <Highlight color="yellow">**NameNode**</Highlight> is the <Highlight color="green">master node</Highlight>. It stores <Highlight color="blue">metadata</Highlight> such as file names, directory structure, permissions, and the mapping of blocks to DataNodes.
- <Highlight color="yellow">**DataNode**</Highlight> is the <Highlight color="green">worker node</Highlight>. It stores the <Highlight color="blue">actual file blocks</Highlight> and performs read and write operations for clients.
- <Highlight color="yellow">**Secondary NameNode**</Highlight> periodically creates <Highlight color="blue">checkpoints of metadata</Highlight> to keep the NameNode's metadata manageable. It is <Highlight color="red">not a direct backup</Highlight> of the NameNode.

**Important points**

- HDFS stores files as <Highlight color="blue">blocks rather than as complete files</Highlight>.
- Blocks are <Highlight color="green">distributed across DataNodes</Highlight>.
- Each block is <Highlight color="green">replicated, usually three times</Highlight>, for reliability.
- HDFS is designed for <Highlight color="green">**high throughput**</Highlight>, <Highlight color="red">not low-latency access</Highlight>.

**Conclusion**

Thus, HDFS provides reliable distributed storage through <Highlight color="blue">block-based storage, replication, and the coordinated working of NameNode and DataNodes</Highlight>.

### Q12. Explain the purpose of NameNode and DataNode in HDFS.

<Highlight color="yellow">**NameNode**</Highlight> and <Highlight color="yellow">**DataNode**</Highlight> are the two most important components of HDFS. Together they manage the storage of files in a distributed Hadoop cluster.

The <Highlight color="yellow">**NameNode**</Highlight> acts as the <Highlight color="green">master of HDFS</Highlight>. Its main job is to <Highlight color="blue">manage metadata</Highlight>. It keeps track of <Highlight color="blue">file names, directory structure, permissions, and the location of file blocks</Highlight> in the cluster. It <Highlight color="red">does not store user data itself</Highlight>, but stores all the information required to locate and manage that data.

The <Highlight color="yellow">**DataNode**</Highlight> acts as the <Highlight color="green">worker node</Highlight>. It <Highlight color="blue">stores the actual data blocks</Highlight> of files and performs the read and write operations requested by the client. DataNodes also send <Highlight color="blue">heartbeat signals</Highlight> to the NameNode to show that they are active. If a DataNode fails, the NameNode <Highlight color="green">arranges replication of its lost blocks</Highlight> using other available DataNodes.

**Summary of Roles**

- <Highlight color="yellow">**NameNode**</Highlight>: <Highlight color="blue">Metadata management and control</Highlight>.
- <Highlight color="yellow">**DataNode**</Highlight>: <Highlight color="green">Actual storage and data operations</Highlight>.

**Conclusion**

Hence, the NameNode acts as the <Highlight color="blue">**brain of HDFS**</Highlight>, while the DataNodes act as the <Highlight color="green">**storage workers that hold the actual file data**</Highlight>.

### Q13. Explain how files are written and read in HDFS.

In HDFS, files are <Highlight color="red">not stored as a single unit</Highlight>. Instead, each file is <Highlight color="blue">divided into large blocks</Highlight>, and these blocks are stored across multiple DataNodes. Every block is <Highlight color="green">replicated on more than one node</Highlight> for fault tolerance.

During the <Highlight color="yellow">**write operation**</Highlight>:
1. The client sends a request to the <Highlight color="blue">NameNode to create a file</Highlight>.
2. The NameNode checks permissions and returns the list of DataNodes.
3. The client writes data to the first DataNode, which then <Highlight color="green">forwards the block via the replication pipeline</Highlight>.
4. After all replicas are stored, <Highlight color="green">acknowledgment is sent back</Highlight> to the client.

During the <Highlight color="yellow">**read operation**</Highlight>:
1. The client contacts the <Highlight color="blue">NameNode and asks for block locations</Highlight>.
2. The NameNode responds with the list of DataNodes.
3. The client reads data directly from the <Highlight color="green">nearest available DataNode</Highlight> and assembles all blocks.

**Conclusion**

Thus, HDFS writes files through a <Highlight color="blue">replication pipeline</Highlight> and reads files directly from DataNodes, making storage <Highlight color="green">reliable and access efficient</Highlight>.

### Q11. Explain Google File System.

The <Highlight color="yellow">**Google File System (GFS)**</Highlight> is a <Highlight color="blue">proprietary distributed file system</Highlight> developed by Google to provide <Highlight color="green">efficient, reliable access to large volumes of data</Highlight> using clusters of commodity hardware. It was designed to handle files that are several terabytes in size and optimized for <Highlight color="green">large sequential reads and data appends</Highlight>.

GFS operates on a <Highlight color="yellow">**Master-Slave architecture**</Highlight>, where a single <Highlight color="green">Master node manages all metadata</Highlight>, while multiple <Highlight color="blue">ChunkServers store the actual data</Highlight>. Files are divided into <Highlight color="blue">fixed-size chunks (64MB)</Highlight>, and each chunk is <Highlight color="green">replicated across at least three ChunkServers</Highlight> to ensure reliability and fault tolerance.

The design of GFS focuses on <Highlight color="green">**High Throughput**</Highlight> rather than <Highlight color="red">low latency</Highlight>, making it suitable for data-intensive processing tasks. It assumes that <Highlight color="red">hardware failure is the norm</Highlight> rather than the exception, and thus includes <Highlight color="green">robust automated recovery mechanisms</Highlight>.

Hence, GFS provided the <Highlight color="blue">architectural blueprint for HDFS</Highlight> and remains a milestone in the development of distributed storage systems.
