### Q1. Predict Graduate School Admission Probability Using a Probit Model <QuestionDot color="yellow" text="IA1-QB" /> <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" />

**Outcome variable**
* $Y = 1$: Admitted
* $Y = 0$: Rejected

**Predictors**
* $X_1$: GPA (0.0-4.0)
* $X_2$: Top-Tier School (1 = Yes, 0 = No)

**Estimated Probit Coefficients**
* Intercept: $\beta_0 = -3.5$
* GPA: $\beta_1 = 1.0$
* Top-Tier School: $\beta_2 = 0.4$
</LongQuestion>

<Callout type="info">
- Solution requires values from Given Z-Table.
- Or use **CASIO fx-991ES PLUS (2nd Edition)**: `MODE` -> `STAT` (3) -> `1: 1-VAR` -> `AC` -> `SHIFT` + `1` -> `5: Distr` -> `1:P(`
</Callout>

#### Model:
$$
P(Y=1 \mid X) = \Phi(Z) \quad \text{where} \quad Z = -3.5 + 1.0X_1 + 0.4X_2
$$

---

#### Case 1: GPA = 3.0, Not Top-Tier ($X_2 = 0$)
$$
Z = -3.5 + 1.0(3.0) + 0.4(0) = -0.5
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Probability = 0.31**</Highlight> $\rightarrow$ **Rejected**

---

#### Case 2: GPA = 3.0, Top-Tier ($X_2 = 1$)
$$
Z = -3.5 + 1.0(3.0) + 0.4(1) = -0.1
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Probability = 0.46**</Highlight> $\rightarrow$ **Rejected (borderline)**

---

#### Case 3: GPA = 4.0, Top-Tier ($X_2 = 1$)
$$
Z = -3.5 + 1.0(4.0) + 0.4(1) = 0.9
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Probability = 0.82**</Highlight> $\rightarrow$ **Admitted**

### Q2. Predict Loan Default Probability Using Logistic and Probit Models <QuestionDot color="yellow" text="IA1-QB" />

**Standardized income score** $x = 1$

**Given coefficients:**
- **Logistic:** $\beta_0 = -0.5$, $\beta_1 = 1.2$
- **Probit:** $\beta_0 = -0.3$, $\beta_1 = 0.7$
</LongQuestion>

<div className="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-8">
<div>
### Logistic Regression
$$
Z = -0.5 + 1.2(1) = 0.7
$$
$$
P(Y=1) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-0.7}} \approx \frac{1}{1 + 0.496} \approx 0.668
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Probability $\approx$ 0.67**</Highlight> $\rightarrow$ **Defaults**
</div>

<div>
### Probit Regression
$$
Z = -0.3 + 0.7(1) = 0.4
$$
$$
P(Y=1) = \Phi(0.4) \approx 0.655
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Probability $\approx$ 0.66**</Highlight> $\rightarrow$ **Defaults**
</div>
</div>

### Q3. COVID-19 Public Health Interpretation <QuestionDot color="cyan" text="MSE-QB" />

A researcher models daily COVID-19 cases using a Poisson GLM with a **log link**.
Predictors: Temperature, Vaccination Rate, Public Holiday.

**Task:** If the coefficient for **vaccination rate** is estimated as **−0.05**, interpret this value.
</LongQuestion>

#### Interpretation:
Since the link is **log**, coefficients are multiplicative on the rate $\lambda$:
$$
e^{-0.05} \approx 0.951
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Result:** Each 1% increase in vaccination rate is associated with a **4.9% decrease** in expected daily cases, holding other factors constant.</Highlight>

### Q4. Environmental Impact Study DAG <QuestionDot color="red" text="Important" />

**Scenario:**
- Industrial Activity (I) affects Air (A) and Water (W).
- Air affects Respiratory Health (R).
- Water affects Diseases (D).
- R and D affect Healthcare Demand (H).
- H affects Gov Expenditure (G).

**Task:** Draw DAG, write joint distribution, and identify 2 conditional independencies.
</LongQuestion>

#### (i) DAG Structure:
<Mermaid chart={`graph LR
  I[Industrial Activity] --> A[Air Quality]
  I --> W[Water Quality]
  A --> R[Respiratory Health]
  W --> D[Waterborne Diseases]
  R --> H[Healthcare Demand]
  D --> H
  H --> G[Government Expenditure]
`} />

#### (ii) Joint Probability Factorization:
$$
P(I, A, W, R, D, H, G) = P(I) P(A \mid I) P(W \mid I) P(R \mid A) P(D \mid W) P(H \mid R, D) P(G \mid H)
$$

#### (iii) Two Conditional Independence Relations:
1. **$R \perp D \mid I$** - Respiratory Health and Waterborne Diseases are independent given Industrial Activity.
2. **$A \perp W \mid I$** - Air Quality and Water Quality are independent given Industrial Activity.

### Q8. Parameter Count in Markov Chains and DGMs <QuestionDot color="yellow" text="IA1-QB" /> <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" />

**Scenario:** A sequence consists of **5 binary variables**.
- (a) Parameters for a **first-order Markov chain**.
- (b) Parameters for a **DGM where each node has two parents**.
</LongQuestion>

#### (a) First-Order Markov Chain
Structure: $X_1 \to X_2 \to X_3 \to X_4 \to X_5$
1. **Initial $P(X_1)$:** $2^0 \times (2-1) = 1$ parameter.
2. **Transitions ($X_2 \dots X_5$):** 4 transitions, each with 1 parent.
   - Per transition: $2^1 \times (2-1) = 2$ parameters.
   - Total transitions: $4 \times 2 = 8$ parameters.

**Total:** $1 + 8 = 9$ parameters.

---

#### (b) DGM with 2 Parents per Node
For 5 nodes, each having 2 parents:
1. Per node configurations: $2^2 = 4$.
2. Independent parameters per configuration: $2 - 1 = 1$.
3. Total per node: $4 \times 1 = 4$ parameters.

**Total for 5 nodes:** $5 \times 4 = 20$ parameters.

### Q9. Responsibility Calculation in GMM <QuestionDot color="cyan" text="MSE-QB" />

For a two-component GMM:
$$
P(x) = \pi_1 \mathcal{N}(x \mid \mu_1, \sigma_1^2) + \pi_2 \mathcal{N}(x \mid \mu_2, \sigma_2^2)
$$
Define and write the formula for **responsibility** $\gamma_{ik}$.
</LongQuestion>

#### Formula:
$$
\gamma_{ik} = \frac{\pi_k \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_k, \sigma_k^2)}{\pi_1 \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_1, \sigma_1^2) + \pi_2 \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_2, \sigma_2^2)}
$$
<Highlight color="green">**Meaning:** The probability that data point $x_i$ was generated by component $k$.</Highlight>
