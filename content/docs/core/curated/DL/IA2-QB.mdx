### Q1. An RNN has input size 15, hidden state size 30, and processes a sequence of length 20 with batch size 64. Calculate the number of multiplications in one forward pass of the entire sequence.
</LongQuestion>

**Given:**

$$
\text{Input size} = 15,\quad \text{Hidden size} = 30,\quad T = 20,\quad \text{Batch size} = 64
$$

**Formula:**

$$
\text{Multiplications per step} = \left[(I \times H + H \times H) \times B\right]
$$

**Step 1:**

$$
\left[x_t W_{xh} = 15 \times 30,\quad h_{t-1}W_{hh} = 30 \times 30\right]
$$

$$
(15 \times 30) + (30 \times 30) = 450 + 900 = 1350
$$

**Step 2:**

$$
1350 \times 64 = 86{,}400
$$

**Step 3:**

$$
86{,}400 \times 20 = 1{,}728{,}000
$$

**Final Answer:**

$$
\boxed{1{,}728{,}000 \text{ multiplications}}
$$

<LongQuestion>
### Q4-Q5. A denoising autoencoder has input $[1,2,3]$, noisy input $[1.2,1.8,3.1]$, encoder weights
$$
W_e = \begin{bmatrix}
0.2 & 0.3 & 0.1 \\
0.1 & 0.2 & 0.3
\end{bmatrix}
$$
and decoder weights
$$
W_d = \begin{bmatrix}
0.4 & 0.1 \\
0.2 & 0.2 \\
0.1 & 0.3
\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Calculate the latent representation and reconstructed output.
</LongQuestion>

Let noisy input be:

$$
\tilde{x}=\begin{bmatrix}1.2 \\ 1.8 \\ 3.1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Step 1: Latent representation using $h=W_e\tilde{x}$:

$$
h = W_e\tilde{x}
= \begin{bmatrix}
0.2 & 0.3 & 0.1 \\
0.1 & 0.2 & 0.3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}1.2 \\ 1.8 \\ 3.1\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix}1.09 \\ 1.41\end{bmatrix}
$$

Component check:

$$
h_1=0.2(1.2)+0.3(1.8)+0.1(3.1)=1.09
$$

$$
h_2=0.1(1.2)+0.2(1.8)+0.3(3.1)=1.41
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{h=[1.09,\;1.41]}
$$

Step 2: Reconstruction using $\hat{x}=W_dh$:

$$
\hat{x}=W_d h
= \begin{bmatrix}
0.4 & 0.1 \\
0.2 & 0.2 \\
0.1 & 0.3
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}1.09 \\ 1.41\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix}0.577 \\ 0.500 \\ 0.532\end{bmatrix}
$$

Component check:

$$
\hat{x}_1=0.4(1.09)+0.1(1.41)=0.577
$$

$$
\hat{x}_2=0.2(1.09)+0.2(1.41)=0.500
$$

$$
\hat{x}_3=0.1(1.09)+0.3(1.41)=0.532
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{\hat{x}=[0.577,\;0.500,\;0.532]}
$$

### Q6. An RNN processes sequences of length 100, with input size 50, hidden state size 128, and batch size 32. If each training step takes $0.8\text{ ms}$ per time step per batch, compute the total time for one epoch with 1,000 batches.
</LongQuestion>

Given:

$$
T=100,\quad \text{time per step per batch}=0.8\text{ ms},\quad \text{batches per epoch}=1000
$$

Step 1: Time for one batch:

$$
100 \times 0.8\text{ ms} = 80\text{ ms}
$$

Step 2: Time for one epoch:

$$
1000 \times 80\text{ ms} = 80{,}000\text{ ms} = 80\text{ s}
$$

Step 3: Convert to minutes:

$$
80\text{ s} = 1\text{ min }20\text{ s}
$$

Final answer: $\boxed{80\text{ s} \; (\approx 1\text{ min }20\text{ s})}$.

### Q8. An undercomplete autoencoder has input layer with 4 neurons and bottleneck layer with 2 neurons. If encoder weights are a $4\times2$ matrix and input is $[1,2,3,4]$, show the matrix multiplication.
</LongQuestion>

Let

$$
x = [1\;2\;3\;4], \qquad
W_e = \begin{bmatrix}
w_{11} & w_{12} \\
w_{21} & w_{22} \\
w_{31} & w_{32} \\
w_{41} & w_{42}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then latent vector is:

$$
h = xW_e
= [1\;2\;3\;4]
\begin{bmatrix}
w_{11} & w_{12} \\
w_{21} & w_{22} \\
w_{31} & w_{32} \\
w_{41} & w_{42}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\Rightarrow h = \big[1w_{11}+2w_{21}+3w_{31}+4w_{41},\;1w_{12}+2w_{22}+3w_{32}+4w_{42}\big]
$$

So output shape is $1\times2$ and this is the exact encoder multiplication pattern asked in the question.

<LongQuestion>
### Q10. Explain the role of deep learning in ImageNet detection and classification.
</LongQuestion>

Deep learning transformed ImageNet by learning hierarchical visual features directly from pixels.

1. <Highlight color="blue">**Classification breakthrough:**</Highlight> CNNs such as AlexNet, VGG, and ResNet drastically improved Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy.
2. <Highlight color="green">**Feature learning:**</Highlight> Early layers learn edges/textures, deeper layers learn object parts and full object semantics.
3. <Highlight color="orange">**Transfer learning:**</Highlight> ImageNet pretraining provides strong feature extractors for medical imaging, remote sensing, and industrial vision tasks.
4. <Highlight color="cyan">**Detection improvement:**</Highlight> Detection models (R-CNN, Faster R-CNN, YOLO, SSD) use deep backbones to localize and classify objects.
5. <Highlight color="green">**Scale and generalization:**</Highlight> Training on millions of labeled images improves robustness to pose, illumination, and viewpoint variation.

In short, ImageNet made deep CNNs the standard foundation for modern image classification and object detection.

### Q11. What is WaveNet? How is it used for audio generation?
</LongQuestion>

<Highlight color="blue">**WaveNet**</Highlight> is a deep generative model for raw audio waveforms developed by DeepMind.

Core idea:

$$
p(x)=\prod_t p(x_t\mid x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{t-1})
$$

It generates one audio sample at a time using:

1. <Highlight color="orange">**Causal convolutions**</Highlight> (no future leakage).
2. <Highlight color="orange">**Dilated convolutions**</Highlight> (large receptive field without huge depth).
3. <Highlight color="green">**Autoregressive sampling**</Highlight> for realistic speech/music waveform synthesis.

Use cases: text-to-speech vocoders, expressive speech synthesis, and neural audio generation.

<LongQuestion>
### Q12. Describe the concept of Word2Vec in Natural Language Processing.
</LongQuestion>

<Highlight color="blue">**Word2Vec**</Highlight> learns dense vector representations (embeddings) of words such that semantically similar words lie close in vector space.

Two training variants:

1. <Highlight color="green">**CBOW:**</Highlight> Predict center word from context words.
2. <Highlight color="green">**Skip-gram:**</Highlight> Predict context words from center word.

Key benefits:

- Captures semantic similarity (king close to queen, car close to vehicle).
- Supports analogies (king - man + woman approx queen).
- Provides compact features for downstream NLP tasks.

<LongQuestion>
### Q13. How is deep learning applied in face recognition systems?
</LongQuestion>

Typical deep-learning face recognition pipeline:

1. Face detection (locate face region).
2. Face alignment (normalize pose/landmarks).
3. Feature extraction using CNN to obtain embedding (for example 128-D).
4. Similarity matching against stored embeddings using cosine or Euclidean distance.
5. Decision: verification (1:1) or identification (1:N).

Models often use contrastive/triplet losses to keep same-person embeddings close and different-person embeddings far apart.

<LongQuestion>
### Q14. Define semantic segmentation with an example.
</LongQuestion>

<Highlight color="blue">**Semantic segmentation**</Highlight> is a deep-learning task in which <Highlight color="green">**every pixel of an image is assigned a semantic class**</Highlight> (for example: road, car, sky, person, tree).

In other words, the model performs <Highlight color="orange">**dense prediction**</Highlight> and produces a class map of size $H \times W$ for an input image of size $H \times W$.

### Key Characteristics

1. Pixel-level output instead of only image-level labels.
2. Objects are identified by class, not by individual instance ID.
3. Output is generally a segmentation mask (color-coded label map).
4. Widely implemented using encoder-decoder networks such as FCN, U-Net, and DeepLab.

### How It Works (Basic Idea)

1. The encoder extracts multi-scale visual features.
2. The decoder upsamples these features to original image resolution.
3. A classifier predicts the class of each pixel.
4. Final mask groups all pixels belonging to the same class.

### Example

In autonomous driving, a street image is segmented into classes like road, lane marking, sky, vehicle, pedestrian, and vegetation.

If there are two cars, semantic segmentation marks both under the same class "car" (it does not separate them as car-1 and car-2). This helps in scene understanding for path planning and obstacle awareness.

### Conclusion

Semantic segmentation provides complete scene-level understanding by telling <Highlight color="green">**what is present at each pixel location**</Highlight>, which is more informative than only bounding-box based detection for many safety-critical applications.

<LongQuestion>
### Q16. Differentiate between image classification and object detection.
</LongQuestion>

<Highlight color="blue">**Image classification**</Highlight> predicts the category of the entire image.

<Highlight color="blue">**Object detection**</Highlight> not only predicts object categories but also gives their spatial locations in the image.

### Detailed Differentiation

| Basis | Image Classification | Object Detection |
|------|----------------------|------------------|
| Core task | Assign class to whole image | Detect each object instance and localize it |
| Question answered | "What is in this image?" | "What objects are present and where are they?" |
| Output format | Class label(s) with probability | Bounding box + class label + confidence score |
| Localization | Not required | Mandatory |
| Annotation needed for training | Image-level labels | Box-level labels (x, y, w, h) |
| Difficulty level | Relatively simpler | More complex (classification + localization) |
| Typical loss | Cross-entropy | Classification loss + localization loss (for box regression) |
| Common metrics | Accuracy, Top-1, Top-5 | mAP, IoU, Precision-Recall |
| Compute requirement | Lower | Higher |
| Typical models | ResNet, EfficientNet, ViT | Faster R-CNN, YOLO, SSD, DETR |
| Typical use cases | Plant disease type, animal species, document category | Traffic monitoring, surveillance, medical lesion localization |

### Example (Exam-Oriented)

Consider a road image containing 2 cars and 1 pedestrian.

1. Image classification output: "road scene" or "contains vehicles".
2. Object detection output:
	- Car: $(x_1,y_1,w_1,h_1)$
	- Car: $(x_2,y_2,w_2,h_2)$
	- Pedestrian: $(x_3,y_3,w_3,h_3)$

Thus, detection provides instance-level understanding, while classification provides only image-level understanding.

### Conclusion

Image classification is suitable when only category identification is needed. Object detection is preferred when both recognition and precise location are required for decision-making systems.

### Q17. A sparse autoencoder uses L1 regularization with $\lambda=0.1$. Latent representation is $h=[0.5,-0.2,0.8]$. Calculate the L1 regularization term added to loss.
</LongQuestion>

Given:

$$
\lambda=0.1,\qquad h=[0.5,-0.2,0.8]
$$

L1 regularization term:

$$
\lambda \sum_i |h_i| = 0.1\left(|0.5| + |-0.2| + |0.8|\right)
$$

Now evaluate the magnitudes:

$$
|0.5|=0.5,\qquad |-0.2|=0.2,\qquad |0.8|=0.8
$$

So,

$$
= 0.1(0.5+0.2+0.8)=0.1(1.5)=\boxed{0.15}
$$

Final answer: L1 term added to loss is $\boxed{0.15}$.

### Q18. An RNN has $W_{hh}=0.4$, $W_{xh}=0.2$, $W_{hy}=0.6$, $b_h=0.1$, $b_y=0.2$, input sequence $[1,2]$, and $h_0=0$.

i) Compute $h_1,h_2,y_1,y_2$ using tanh hidden activation and linear output.

ii) Compute error at second time step if target output is $1$.
</LongQuestion>

RNN equations:

$$
h_t = \tanh(W_{xh}x_t + W_{hh}h_{t-1} + b_h),\qquad y_t = W_{hy}h_t + b_y
$$

Given values:

$$
W_{hh}=0.4,\;W_{xh}=0.2,\;W_{hy}=0.6,\;b_h=0.1,\;b_y=0.2,\;h_0=0
$$

For $t=1$:

$$
h_1 = \tanh(0.2\cdot1 + 0.4\cdot0 + 0.1) = \tanh(0.3) \approx 0.2913
$$
$$
y_1 = 0.6(0.2913) + 0.2 \approx 0.3748
$$

For $t=2$:

$$
h_2 = \tanh(0.2\cdot2 + 0.4\cdot0.2913 + 0.1)
= \tanh(0.6165) \approx 0.5487
$$
$$
y_2 = 0.6(0.5487) + 0.2 \approx 0.5292
$$

So,

$$
\boxed{h_1\approx0.2913,\;h_2\approx0.5487,\;y_1\approx0.3748,\;y_2\approx0.5292}
$$

If target at $t=2$ is $t_2=1$:

$$
e_2 = t_2 - y_2 = 1 - 0.5292 = \boxed{0.4708}
$$

If squared error is asked:

$$
e_2^2 \approx 0.2217,\qquad \frac{1}{2}e_2^2 \approx 0.1108
$$

Final answer:

$$
\boxed{h_1\approx0.2913,\;h_2\approx0.5487,\;y_1\approx0.3748,\;y_2\approx0.5292,\;e_2\approx0.4708}
$$

### Q20. Given input sequence $[1,2,3]$ and simple RNN weight $W_{hh}=0.2$, show first two steps of unrolling if $W_{xh}=0$, $b_h=0$, and $h_0=0$.
</LongQuestion>

General hidden update:

$$
h_t = \tanh(W_{xh}x_t + W_{hh}h_{t-1} + b_h)
$$

Given:

$$
W_{xh}=0,\;W_{hh}=0.2,\;b_h=0,\;h_0=0
$$

Given $W_{xh}=0$ and $b_h=0$, this simplifies to:

$$
h_t = \tanh(0.2h_{t-1})
$$

For first time step ($x_1=1$, though it has no effect because $W_{xh}=0$):

$$
z_1 = 0.2h_0 = 0.2(0)=0
$$

$$
h_1 = \tanh(z_1)=\tanh(0)=0
$$

For second time step ($x_2=2$, again no effect because $W_{xh}=0$):

$$
z_2 = 0.2h_1 = 0.2(0)=0
$$

$$
h_2 = \tanh(z_2)=\tanh(0)=0
$$

Hence first two hidden states are:

$$
\boxed{h_1=0,\;h_2=0}
$$

(And similarly $h_3=0$ for this setup.)
