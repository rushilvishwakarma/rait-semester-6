### Q2. Explain shallow and deep neural networks along with relevant deep learning terminologies. <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" />

#### Shallow and Deep Neural Networks

A <Highlight color="blue">**shallow neural network**</Highlight> contains only one hidden layer between the input and output layers. It is used for learning simple patterns and requires less computational power.

A <Highlight color="blue">**deep neural network**</Highlight> contains two or more hidden layers. The multiple layers enable it to learn complex and hierarchical representations of data. Deep networks require large datasets and higher computational resources.

The main difference between them is the <Highlight>**number of hidden layers**</Highlight> and learning capacity. Shallow networks are suitable for simple problems, whereas deep networks handle complex tasks.

#### Relevant Deep Learning Terminologies

A <Highlight color="cyan">**neuron**</Highlight> is the basic processing unit. <Highlight color="cyan">**Weights**</Highlight> determine the importance of inputs, and <Highlight color="cyan">**bias**</Highlight> adjusts the output. An <Highlight color="cyan">**activation function**</Highlight> introduces non-linearity. The <Highlight color="cyan">**loss function**</Highlight> measures prediction error. <Highlight color="cyan">**Backpropagation**</Highlight> updates weights using gradient descent.

### Q4. Explain the bias–variance tradeoff in machine learning. <QuestionDot color="blue" text="Asked in MSE" /> 

#### Bias–Variance Tradeoff in Machine Learning

The <Highlight color="blue">**bias–variance tradeoff**</Highlight> describes the balance between two types of errors that affect the performance of a machine learning model: <Highlight color="orange">**bias error**</Highlight> and <Highlight color="orange">**variance error**</Highlight>.

**Bias** refers to the error caused by <Highlight color="red">**overly simple assumptions**</Highlight> in the model. A model with high bias fails to capture the underlying pattern of the data and leads to <Highlight color="red">**underfitting**</Highlight>. Such models perform poorly on both training and testing data.

**Variance** refers to the error caused by <Highlight color="red">**excessive sensitivity to small fluctuations**</Highlight> in the training data. A model with high variance learns the training data too closely, including noise, and leads to <Highlight color="red">**overfitting**</Highlight>. Such models perform well on training data but poorly on unseen data.

The tradeoff arises because <Highlight>**reducing bias usually increases variance, and reducing variance usually increases bias**</Highlight>. An ideal model achieves a balance where both bias and variance are minimized to obtain good generalization performance.

Thus, the goal in machine learning is to select a model complexity that provides the <Highlight color="green">**optimal balance between bias and variance**</Highlight>, resulting in minimum prediction error on new data.

### Q5. Discuss the role of L1 and L2 regularization and early stopping in controlling overfitting. <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" /> <QuestionDot color="blue" text="Asked in MSE" />

### Q12. Describe regularization techniques in Deep learning (early stopping, dataset augmentation, parameter sharing, injecting noise into input, ensemble methods, dropout,)
</LongQuestion>

Deep learning models often suffer from <Highlight color="red">**overfitting**</Highlight> and <Highlight color="red">**slow convergence**</Highlight>. <Highlight color="blue">**Regularization techniques**</Highlight> help reduce overfitting by controlling model complexity, while <Highlight color="blue">**optimization techniques**</Highlight> help minimize the loss function efficiently during training.

Let the prediction of a model be:

$$
\hat{y} = w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots + w_m x_m + b
$$

where $w_j$ are weights and $b$ is bias.

Let the original loss function (Mean Squared Error) be:

$$
L = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2
$$

---

#### Regularization Techniques

**L1 Regularization (Lasso):**

$$
L_{total} = L + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{m} |w_j|
$$

It adds a penalty equal to the absolute values of weights.
It <Highlight color="green">**forces some weights to become zero**</Highlight>, performs feature selection, and reduces overfitting.

**L2 Regularization (Ridge):**

$$
L_{total} = L + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j^2
$$

It adds a penalty equal to squared weights.
It <Highlight color="green">**reduces large weight values**</Highlight> and prevents the model from becoming too sensitive to training data.

**Early Stopping:**

Training is stopped when <Highlight color="orange">**validation loss starts increasing**</Highlight> while training loss continues decreasing.
This prevents the model from learning noise in the training data.

**Dropout:**

<Highlight color="orange">**Randomly deactivates neurons**</Highlight> during training, reducing co-adaptation and forcing the model to learn redundant representations.

**Dataset Augmentation:**

Artificially increases the size and diversity of the training set by applying transformations like <Highlight color="orange">**rotation, flipping, and scaling**</Highlight>. It prevents the model from memorizing specific training samples.

**Parameter Sharing:**

The same set of weights is reused across different parts of a model (e.g., in <Highlight color="green">**CNN filters**</Highlight>). This significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters and controls model complexity.

**Injecting Noise into Input:**

Adding small random perturbations (<Highlight color="orange">**Gaussian noise**</Highlight>) to input data during training. It forces the model to learn stable patterns and improves robustness.

**Ensemble Methods:**

Combining predictions from <Highlight color="green">**multiple independently trained models**</Highlight> (via averaging or voting). It reduces variance as errors from individual models tend to cancel out.

---

#### Optimization Techniques

**Gradient Descent:**

$$
w = w - \eta \frac{\partial L}{\partial w}
$$

where $\eta$ is the <Highlight color="orange">**learning rate**</Highlight>.
It updates weights in the direction of minimum loss.

**Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD):**

Updates weights using <Highlight color="green">**one training sample at a time**</Highlight> for faster convergence.

**Adam Optimizer:**

An <Highlight color="green">**adaptive optimization algorithm**</Highlight> that adjusts learning rates for each parameter and improves convergence speed.

---

#### Conclusion

Regularization techniques such as L1, L2, Early Stopping, Dropout, Dataset Augmentation, Parameter Sharing, Noise Injection, and Ensemble Methods control overfitting by limiting model complexity and improving robustness. Optimization techniques such as Gradient Descent, SGD, and Adam efficiently minimize the loss function. Together, they ensure stable and effective training of deep learning models.

### Q6. How does dataset augmentation improve model generalization? / How does data augmentation compare to regularization in preventing overfitting? <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" />

<Highlight color="blue">**Dataset augmentation**</Highlight> is a technique used to artificially increase the size and diversity of a training dataset by applying transformations to existing data samples. It helps improve <Highlight color="green">**model generalization**</Highlight> by reducing overfitting and making the model more robust.

In dataset augmentation, new training examples are created using transformations such as <Highlight color="orange">**rotation, flipping, scaling, cropping, noise addition, or brightness adjustment**</Highlight> (in images). In text data, augmentation may include <Highlight color="orange">**synonym replacement or paraphrasing**</Highlight>.

Dataset augmentation improves generalization in the following ways:

1. **Increases Training Data Size:** By generating more variations of existing data, the model is exposed to more examples, reducing the chance of memorizing the training set.

2. **Reduces Overfitting:** Since the model sees multiple variations of the same data, it learns general patterns instead of specific details or noise.

3. **Improves Robustness:** The model becomes invariant to small changes in input, such as rotation or lighting in images.

4. **Acts as Regularization:** Augmentation introduces controlled randomness, which behaves like a regularization technique and reduces variance.

Thus, dataset augmentation enhances the model’s ability to perform well on unseen data by improving diversity, reducing variance, and encouraging learning of meaningful patterns rather than noise.

### Q7. Explain parameter sharing and injecting noise into input as regularization techniques in deep learning.

Overfitting occurs when a deep learning model learns noise from training data and fails to generalize. Regularization techniques such as **parameter sharing** and **injecting noise into input** help reduce overfitting and improve generalization.

---

#### 1. Parameter Sharing

<Highlight color="blue">**Parameter sharing**</Highlight> is a technique where the same set of weights is used across different parts of a model instead of learning separate weights for each connection.

For example, in <Highlight color="green">**Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**</Highlight>, a single filter (set of weights) is applied across different regions of an image. Instead of learning separate weights for every pixel location, the <Highlight>**same weights are reused**</Highlight>.

If a filter has weights $w$, the same $w$ is applied at every spatial location.

**Role in Regularization:**

* Reduces the total number of parameters
* Decreases model complexity
* Prevents overfitting
* Improves generalization

By reducing the number of free parameters, the model becomes less likely to memorize training data.

---

#### 2. Injecting Noise into Input

<Highlight color="blue">**Injecting noise**</Highlight> involves adding small random perturbations to input data during training.

If input is $x$, noisy input becomes:

$$
x' = x + \epsilon
$$

where $\epsilon$ is small random noise (e.g., Gaussian noise).

**Role in Regularization:**

* Forces the model to learn stable patterns
* Prevents reliance on exact input values
* Improves robustness
* Acts similar to data augmentation

The model learns to make correct predictions even when inputs slightly vary, which improves generalization.

---

#### Conclusion

Parameter sharing reduces the number of trainable parameters, thereby controlling model complexity. Injecting noise introduces variability during training, making the model robust to small changes. Both techniques act as regularization methods to reduce overfitting and improve generalization performance.

### Q9. What is greedy layer-wise pre-training? 

<Highlight color="blue">**Greedy layer-wise pre-training**</Highlight> is a training strategy used in deep neural networks where each layer is trained **individually and sequentially**, one layer at a time, instead of training the entire network at once.

It is called **“greedy”** because each layer is trained independently to optimize its own objective before moving to the next layer.

---

#### Working Procedure

1. The first hidden layer is trained using the input data.
2. Once trained, its <Highlight>**weights are fixed**</Highlight>.
3. The output of the first layer becomes the input to the second layer.
4. The second layer is trained.
5. This process continues layer by layer.
6. Finally, the entire network is <Highlight color="green">**fine-tuned using supervised learning**</Highlight> (e.g., backpropagation).

---

#### Why It Is Used

* Helps in training very deep networks
* Provides good weight initialization
* Reduces problems like vanishing gradients
* Improves convergence

This method was widely used before modern techniques like better initialization methods, ReLU activation, and Batch Normalization became common.

---

#### Conclusion

Greedy layer-wise pre-training trains a deep network one layer at a time to learn meaningful feature representations before performing global fine-tuning, thereby improving stability and performance in deep learning models.

### Q10. Explain its significance in training deep neural networks. Also, discuss the need for advanced activation functions.

#### Greedy Layer-Wise Pre-Training

Greedy layer-wise pre-training is a method used to train deep neural networks by training one layer at a time before training the entire network together. Each layer is trained individually using the output of the previous layer as input. After all layers are pre-trained, the whole network is fine-tuned using backpropagation.

#### Significance in Training Deep Neural Networks

1. <Highlight color="green">**Better Weight Initialization:**</Highlight> It provides good initial values for weights instead of random initialization.
2. <Highlight color="orange">**Prevents Vanishing Gradient Problem:**</Highlight> Earlier deep networks suffered from gradient vanishing; layer-wise training reduces this issue.
3. <Highlight color="green">**Faster Convergence:**</Highlight> Since weights are initialized meaningfully, training becomes more stable and faster.
4. <Highlight color="green">**Improves Generalization:**</Highlight> Pre-training helps the model learn useful feature representations before supervised fine-tuning.
5. <Highlight color="green">**Useful for Limited Labeled Data:**</Highlight> It can be used with unsupervised learning, which is helpful when labeled data is scarce.

Although modern techniques like ReLU activation, Batch Normalization, and improved optimizers have reduced the need for pre-training, it was very important in early deep learning models.

---

#### Need for Advanced Activation Functions

Activation functions introduce non-linearity into neural networks. Without them, the network would behave like a simple linear model regardless of the number of layers.

Earlier activation functions such as <Highlight color="red">**Sigmoid and Tanh**</Highlight> suffer from problems like:

* <Highlight color="red">**Vanishing gradients**</Highlight>
* Slow convergence
* <Highlight color="red">**Saturation at extreme values**</Highlight>

Advanced activation functions such as <Highlight color="green">**ReLU**</Highlight> and its variants address these problems by:

* <Highlight color="green">**Reducing vanishing gradient effects**</Highlight>
* Improving computational efficiency
* <Highlight color="green">**Allowing faster training**</Highlight>
* Enabling deeper networks

Thus, advanced activation functions are necessary to train deep neural networks effectively and achieve better performance.

---

#### Conclusion

Greedy layer-wise pre-training improves stability and initialization in deep networks, while advanced activation functions enable efficient gradient flow and faster convergence. Both contribute significantly to effective training of deep neural networks.

### Q8. Analyze the effectiveness of ensemble methods and dropout in reducing variance in deep learning models. Compare their working principles. / Explain dropout as a regularization technique with its advantages. <QuestionDot color="green" text="Asked in IA1" />

High-variance models tend to overfit the training data. Ensemble methods and dropout reduce variance and improve generalization.

**Ensemble Methods:**
<Highlight color="blue">**Ensemble methods**</Highlight> combine predictions from <Highlight color="green">**multiple independently trained models**</Highlight>. The final output is obtained by <Highlight color="orange">**averaging or majority voting**</Highlight>:

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^{k} f_i(x)
$$

Averaging reduces variance because errors from individual models cancel out.

**Dropout:**
<Highlight color="blue">**Dropout**</Highlight> randomly deactivates a fraction of neurons during training. It prevents <Highlight color="orange">**co-adaptation of neurons**</Highlight> and forces the model to learn robust features. It acts like training <Highlight color="green">**many implicit subnetworks**</Highlight> within a single model.

---

#### Comparison

| Basis              | Ensemble Methods                | Dropout                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Principle          | Combine multiple trained models | Randomly deactivate neurons during training |
| Variance Reduction | Averaging reduces fluctuations  | Implicit averaging of subnetworks           |
| Model Count        | Multiple models                 | Single model                                |
| Computational Cost | High                            | Moderate                                    |
| Usage              | Strong variance reduction       | Efficient regularization technique          |

### Q11. Explain batch normalization in detail. How does it help in faster convergence and address internal covariate shift during training?

<Highlight color="blue">**Batch Normalization**</Highlight>: A technique used to normalize the inputs of each layer in a neural network during training. For every mini-batch, it adjusts the activations so that they have approximately <Highlight color="green">**zero mean and unit variance**</Highlight>, followed by a learnable scaling and shifting step.

<Highlight color="orange">**Internal covariate shift**</Highlight>: During training, as earlier layers update, the distribution of inputs to later layers keeps changing.
Because of this, each layer must continuously adjust to new input distributions → training becomes slow.

Batch normalization reduces this shift by keeping the input distribution of each layer stable. As a result:

* <Highlight color="green">**Training becomes more stable**</Highlight>.
* <Highlight color="green">**Higher learning rates can be used**</Highlight>.
* <Highlight color="green">**Convergence becomes faster**</Highlight>.
* It slightly <Highlight color="green">**reduces overfitting**</Highlight>.

Thus, batch normalization improves training efficiency and accelerates convergence in deep neural networks.

### Q15. List all the types of activation functions. Explain in detail with an example.

<Highlight color="blue">**Activation functions**</Highlight> introduce <Highlight>**non-linearity**</Highlight> into neural networks so that complex patterns can be learned.

---

#### 1) Linear Activation

$$
f(x) = x
$$

Used in <Highlight color="green">**regression problems**</Highlight>.
Example: Predicting house prices.

---

#### 2) Sigmoid Function

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}
$$

Output range: $(0,1)$.
Example: <Highlight color="green">**Binary classification**</Highlight> (spam/not spam).

---

#### 3) Tanh Function

$$
f(x) = \tanh(x)
$$

Output range: $(-1,1)$.
Example: <Highlight color="green">**Hidden layers**</Highlight> in neural networks.

---

#### 4) ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit)

$$
f(x) = \max(0, x)
$$

<Highlight>**Most widely used activation**</Highlight> in deep networks.
Example: Hidden layers in CNNs.

---

#### 5) Softmax Function

$$
f(x_i) = \frac{e^{x_i}}{\sum_{j} e^{x_j}}
$$

Converts outputs into probabilities.
Example: <Highlight color="green">**Multi-class classification**</Highlight>.

---

#### Conclusion

Different activation functions are used depending on the task. <Highlight color="green">**ReLU**</Highlight> is commonly used in hidden layers, while <Highlight color="orange">**Sigmoid**</Highlight> and <Highlight color="orange">**Softmax**</Highlight> are used in output layers.
