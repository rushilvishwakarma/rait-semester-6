### Q3. Explain with examples basic principles of Design of Experiments

**(5 Marks)**

The basic principles of Design of Experiments (DOE) ensure that experiments are 

- **Efficient**
- **Reliable**
- **Scientifically valid**

The first principle is <Highlight color="green">**randomization**</Highlight>. This means doing experiments in a random order so that outside factors do not affect the results too much. For example, running tests randomly can reduce problems caused by machine wear or changes in the environment.

The second principle is <Highlight color="green">**replication**</Highlight>. This means repeating the same experiment several times. Repeating trials helps us see how much the results vary and makes the findings more accurate. For example, testing the strength of a product many times under the same conditions shows how consistent it is.

The third principle is <Highlight color="green">**blocking**</Highlight>. This involves grouping similar experimental units together to reduce the effect of known factors that are not being studied. For example, grouping experiments by machine or work shift helps focus on the main factors being tested.

These principles together improve the reliability and validity of experimental results in industrial applications.

### Q4. Explain in short

- (1) Degree of Freedom (DOF)

- (2) Confounding

- (3) Design of Resolution
</LongQuestion>
**(5 Marks)**

**(1) Degree of Freedom (DOF)**

Degree of freedom is the **number of independent pieces of information** available for estimating a parameter. In DOE, it indicates how many factor effects or errors can be estimated independently.

---

**(2) Confounding**

Confounding occurs when the effects of **two or more factors or interactions are mixed**, so their individual effects cannot be separated. It commonly occurs in fractional factorial designs.

---

**(3) Design of Resolution**

Design resolution shows how much overlap (confounding) exists between effects in a factorial design. A higher design resolution means that **main effects and interactions** are more clearly distinguishable.

### Q5. Explain the importance of selecting quality characteristics for industrial experiments

**(5 Marks)**

Selecting appropriate quality characteristics is a critical step in industrial experiments because it determines **what aspect of performance is being measured and improved**. Quality characteristics represent the response variables that reflect product performance, reliability, or customer satisfaction.

If incorrect or irrelevant quality characteristics are chosen, the experiment may lead to misleading conclusions even if the design is statistically correct. Proper selection ensures that experimental results are **meaningful and aligned with business objectives** such as cost reduction, quality improvement, or process efficiency.

Quality characteristics also help in evaluating the effect of input factors accurately and in comparing different process conditions. In manufacturing, characteristics such as strength, surface finish, dimensional accuracy, and defect rate are commonly used.

Thus, selecting the right quality characteristics is essential for obtaining useful, reliable, and actionable results from industrial experiments.

### Q6. Explain the importance of interactions of different factors in DOE
**(5 or 10 Marks)**

In Design of Experiments (DOE), interaction between factors occurs when the effect of one factor on the response variable depends on the level of another factor. In many industrial processes, factors do not act independently, and therefore studying only main effects may lead to incorrect or incomplete conclusions.

Interactions are important because they help in understanding the combined behavior of multiple factors acting simultaneously in a process. By identifying interactions, engineers can determine which factor combinations significantly influence the response and can select better operating conditions for process improvement.

Studying interactions also helps in identifying optimal factor level combinations rather than focusing on individual factors. It improves process robustness, reduces variability, and enhances product quality. Therefore, analysis of interaction effects is essential in DOE for accurate modeling, reliable optimization, and effective decision-making in industrial experiments.

### Q7. Alternative method for calculating the two-factor (second-order) interaction effect

**(5 Marks)**

The alternative method for calculating a two-factor interaction effect involves using **sign tables or contrast coefficients** instead of graphical analysis. In a two-level factorial experiment, interaction effects are calculated by assigning **+1 and −1 signs** to factor level combinations.

For two factors A and B, the interaction effect AB is obtained by multiplying the signs of factors A and B for each experimental run. The interaction effect is then calculated as the **difference between the average response at +1 level and −1 level of the interaction**, divided by the appropriate normalization factor.

This method is systematic, simple, and well suited for factorial designs, especially when analyzing experiments with multiple factors.

### Q8. Synergistic interaction vs Antagonistic interaction

**(10 Marks)**

Interaction effects in DOE can be classified as **synergistic** or **antagonistic**, depending on how factors influence the response when combined.

**Synergistic Interaction**

A synergistic interaction occurs when the **combined effect of two factors is greater than the sum of their individual effects**. In this case, the factors reinforce each other and produce an enhanced response. For example, increasing both temperature and pressure together may significantly improve product strength more than expected from their individual effects.

Synergistic interactions are desirable in many processes because they lead to improved performance and efficiency.

---

**Antagonistic Interaction**

An antagonistic interaction occurs when the **combined effect of two factors is less than the sum of their individual effects**. Here, one factor reduces or counteracts the effect of the other. For example, increasing temperature may improve yield, but when combined with high speed, the overall yield may decrease.

Antagonistic interactions indicate conflict between factors and help identify combinations that should be avoided.

---

**Conclusion**

Understanding whether interactions are synergistic or antagonistic helps engineers select proper factor combinations, optimize processes, and avoid undesirable operating conditions. Hence, interaction analysis plays a crucial role in effective experimental design.
